Reproducibility and Reliability Issues: 
Algorithms trained on data from one center 
often perform poorly on data from other 
institutions due to differences in equipment 
and imaging protocols. 
Shortcuts and Bias: Algorithms can learn 
from artifacts or random features in the data 
(e.g., image labels) rather than from actual 
pathologies, leading to incorrect predictions 
on new data. 
Black Box and Trust: The uninterpretable 
nature of complex AI models remains a 
major obstacle to their acceptance by 
clinicians. This has spurred a growing 
number of studies on Explainable AI (XAI), 
aimed at visualizing what the algorithm is 
"looking at."

AI has become an essential component of 
modern radiology research but remains 
limited in real-world clinical deployment. 
1) Strengths of Current Models 
• High diagnostic accuracy 
• Reduced reporting time 
• Consistent and reproducible 
measurements 
• Potential to alleviate radiologist 
shortages 
2) Weaknesses / Research Gaps 
• Lack of diverse training datasets 
• Absence of unified evaluation 
standards 
• Need for explainability and trust in 
AI decisions 
• Data privacy challenges 
3) Opportunities 
• Integration of generative AI for 
synthetic datasets 
• Federated learning for privacy-
preserving model training 
• Advanced multi-modal diagnostic 
systems 
4) Threats / Risks 
• Ethical concerns from biased or

• Legal responsibility for AI-

• Overdependence on automated tools

Conclusion 
The field has evolved from initial 
enthusiasm and demonstrations of 
potential to a mature stage characterized 
by a deep understanding of technological 
limitations and systemic challenges. The 
primary focus has shifted from the creation 
   
 
   
 
of pinpoint diagnostic algorithms to the 
reliable integration of AI into clinical 
practice, which requires addressing issues 
of interpretability, reproducibility, data 
bias, and the creation of an appropriate IT 
infrastructure. AI in medical imaging is 
becoming more than just a tool for "image 
enhancement" but a key component of 
future diagnostic, predictive, and 
personalized medicine.
